## Summary
Japanese chaff flower is a perennial plant about 20 to 39 inches tall with square, branching stems and swollen joints. Its tall growth and dense patches shade out and push out many native plants. It grows mainly in floodplains, ditches, bottomland forests and riverbanks with rich, moist soil, but also along roadsides, field edges and vacant urban lots. Its tiny seed parts stick easily to clothing, and it spreads quickly along waterways and trails.

## History
Japanese chaff flower is native to East Asia, mainly Korea and Japan.

## Seasonal differences
It flowers from August to September with spikes of tiny green flowers at the ends of the stems and where the leaves join. The small bracts around the seeds easily stick to cloth.

## How to handle it
In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
