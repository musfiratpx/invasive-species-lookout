## Summary
Autumn olive is a large shrub up to 15 feet tall and 20 feet wide, with dull green leaves that are silvery underneath. It shades out and out-competes native plants. Bacteria in its roots let it pull nitrogen from the air, so it can grow in very poor soil, but this also changes the soil and disrupts its normal nutrient cycle. A single shrub can produce 200,000 seeds in a year, and birds love the berries and spread the seeds.

## History
Autumn olive is native to East Asia.

## Seasonal differences
Autumn olive flowers in early summer. Mature shrubs then produce dense clusters of bright red berries.

## How to handle it
Seedlings can be pulled by hand. Larger plants are cut and the stump treated with herbicide, and follow-up treatments may be needed. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
