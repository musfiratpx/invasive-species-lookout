## Summary
Bohemian knotweed is the hybrid of Japanese knotweed and giant knotweed. The two parents cross widely, so New York's official ranking form assesses them and their hybrid together, and ranks them Very High. It comes up early, grows fast (up to about 3 inches a day) and forms stands that smother everything beneath them. Its dead stems last through the winter, smothering plants below and creating a fire and flood hazard.

## History
Its parent species are native to Asia (China, Japan and Korea). Knotweeds have occasionally been planted for erosion control and sold as garden plants, and they are now found throughout New York State.

## Seasonal differences
It comes up early in the season and grows quickly; last year's dead stems stay standing through the winter.

## How to handle it
Pieces of its stems and roots can grow into new plants, and it is extremely difficult to get rid of once established. Seeds and plant fragments can be carried in soil and compost, and pieces can be carried by water.
