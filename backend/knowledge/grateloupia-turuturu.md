## Summary
Devil's tongue weed is a red seaweed with flat, thick, slippery blades that range from small leaves to blades up to 3 meters long, pink to deep red-brown, with no midrib. It grows in the low tide zone and shallow water, attached to rocks and man-made surfaces. Research from Long Island Sound suggests it may out-compete native seaweeds such as Irish moss and reduce the variety and number of small invertebrates, though its effects appear to be local.

## History
Devil's tongue weed is native to the Northwest Pacific. It was first seen on the East Coast in 1994 in Narragansett Bay, Rhode Island, probably brought in ships' ballast water or on ship hulls. It was first found in New York in 2001 at Montauk Point, and its impacts here are still unknown.

## How to handle it
Do not release it alive. DEC asks people to take several photos from different angles, dispose of the specimen, and report it. Clean, drain and dry boats and equipment before visiting another waterbody, and never dump aquarium contents into waterbodies, ditches or sewers.
