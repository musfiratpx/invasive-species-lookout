## Summary
Chinese silvergrass is a large, clump-forming grass that is widely sold as an ornamental. On New York's invasive species list it is a regulated species: it is legal to buy, sell and grow, but it is illegal to release it into the wild. Some escaped populations stay as one or a few clumps, while others form large stands that spread quickly; on Long Island, stands covering many acres have been seen at Heckscher State Park and Seatuck National Wildlife Refuge. Its dead leaves and stems burn very hot and fast, so it may make fires more frequent and intense.

## History
Chinese silvergrass is native to eastern Asia. It has spread through sales of many garden varieties, and has also been introduced for bioenergy. Its seeds can travel on clothing and in yard waste.

## Seasonal differences
Its seeds have long hairs and are carried by the wind. Flowering times vary a lot between varieties, and some bloom so late that they cannot set seed before frost.

## How to handle it
Small patches can be pulled by hand, but this works less well for large stands. Cutting it close to the ground repeatedly through the growing season for two years kills most plants, though more follow-up may be needed because seeds stay in the soil. Plants in wetlands are harder to remove.
