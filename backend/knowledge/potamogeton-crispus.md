## Summary
Curly pondweed is an underwater plant that can form dense mats in ponds, lakes, streams and brackish waters. Large stands can greatly lower the oxygen in the water, especially when the plants decay, and can block light. Because it grows and dies back earlier in the season than most native plants, its competition with them may be less severe.

## History
Curly pondweed is native to Eurasia. It spreads by underground stems, fragments and buds carried by water and wildlife, and on boats and equipment that aren't properly cleaned.

## Seasonal differences
It grows early in the season, before most native water plants, and decomposes early in the season, while native plants are still growing.

## How to handle it
Once established, it is very hard to get rid of. Cleaning boats and equipment helps stop it from spreading. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
