## Summary
Yellow flag iris is a wetland plant with showy yellow flowers that has invaded stream and pond edges, floodplains, shorelines and marshes. It spreads quickly through thick underground stems and forms dense stands that crowd out cattails and native irises. Its roots form a dense mat that compacts the soil, and large stands can reduce habitat for native fish and waterfowl and clog streams. All parts of the plant are toxic to livestock and other animals.

## History
Yellow flag iris is native to Europe, Asia and northern Africa. It was brought to North America as an ornamental plant as early as the late 1700s, and has since been planted on purpose for gardens, erosion control and sewage treatment ponds. It is now found in all but four U.S. states.

## Seasonal differences
Shoots come up in spring, and in mild winters they can stay green all year. It flowers from late May into early July, though plants usually don't flower until their third year. Seeds form from August through October and can float for more than a year, spreading along waterways.

## How to handle it
All parts of yellow flag iris are toxic to livestock and other animals. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
