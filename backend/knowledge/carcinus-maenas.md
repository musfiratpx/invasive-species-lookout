## Summary
The European green crab is one of the most successful invasive predators of coastal waters, established on five continents. In the Northeast it has harmed soft-shell clams, young oysters and native crabs since the 1950s, and it has been blamed for destroying New England's soft-shell clam fishery and reducing scallops and quahogs. In Connecticut, green crabs caused most of the observed deaths of scallops.

## History
The European green crab is native to the Atlantic coasts of Europe and northern Africa, from Norway and the British Isles to Mauritania. It spreads in ships' ballast and on hulls, in seaweed used to pack live seafood, with shellfish moved for farming, and as drifting larvae.
