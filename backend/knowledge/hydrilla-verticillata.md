## Summary
Hydrilla, also called water thyme, is an underwater plant with long stems, up to 25 feet, that branch at the surface into thick mats. It is one of the world's worst aquatic invasive plants: it can grow in deep, dark water where most native plants can't, its stems can grow up to an inch a day, and its surface mats block sunlight and push out native plants. Its small, pointed leaves grow in rings of 3 to 10 around the stem.

## History
Hydrilla is native to Asia. One form was imported to the U.S. as an aquarium plant in the early 1950s and was soon found in canals in Miami and Tampa; the form found in New York is believed to have come from Korea and was introduced separately decades later in the Potomac River basin.

## Seasonal differences
In New York, hydrilla plants have both male and female flowers, set some seed, and survive the winter as underground tubers.

## How to handle it
Hydrilla spreads mainly by stem fragments, so even tiny pieces can start a new population. It is most often carried to new waters on boats, trailers and live wells, and as a hitchhiker on water-garden plants. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
