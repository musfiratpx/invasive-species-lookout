## Summary
Tartarian honeysuckle is a shrub, usually 6 to 15 feet tall, with small, very fragrant tube-shaped flowers. It forms very dense thickets that crowd out and suppress native plants, and invasive honeysuckle berries are less fatty and nutritious than native ones, which can harm migrating birds that eat lots of them. Invasive honeysuckles have hollow stems, while native honeysuckles have solid stems.

## History
Tartarian honeysuckle is native to Central Asia and southern Russia. It is believed to have been brought to North America as an ornamental as early as the 1750s. It escaped cultivation and is easily spread by birds.

## Seasonal differences
It flowers from May to June with small, very fragrant tube-shaped flowers, from creamy white to shades of pink and crimson. Its quarter-inch red berries appear from mid-summer through early fall.

## How to handle it
Because birds spread the seeds, control should start in late summer or early fall, before the berries are ready. Seedlings and young plants can be removed by hand when there are only a few, repeated every year. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
