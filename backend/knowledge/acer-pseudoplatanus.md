## Summary
Sycamore maple is a tall tree, up to 100 feet, with maple-shaped leaves and scaly, flaky bark that shows a slightly orange layer underneath when peeled. Its seedlings are extremely shade-tolerant and grow fast, so they can out-compete other young plants and turn diverse forests into stands of mostly sycamore maple. It grows along rivers, in disturbed woods, lawns, old fields and roadsides.

## History
Sycamore maple is native to central and southern Europe. It was brought to the northeastern United States as a street and park tree, and by the early 1900s it had been recorded spreading from planted trees into natural areas. It now grows wild in at least 12 states.

## Seasonal differences
Dangling clusters of yellowish-green flowers appear from May to June, followed by paired winged seeds with much broader wings than New York's native maples. A tree starts making seeds at about 25 to 30 years old and can then produce 10,000 to 170,000 seeds, most of which can sprout.

## How to handle it
Small seedlings can be pulled or dug up, but all of the root must come out. Mowing or cutting usually does not kill it and just makes the stem fork. Managed areas need to be checked for several years for new sprouts and seedlings. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
