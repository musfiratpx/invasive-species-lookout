## Summary
Fly honeysuckle (Lonicera × bella) is an invasive honeysuckle shrub on New York's prohibited list. New York's official invasiveness ranking form assesses it together with Morrow's and Tartarian honeysuckle and ranks the group Very High. Invasive honeysuckle shrubs form very dense thickets that crowd out native plants. Invasive honeysuckles have hollow stems, while native honeysuckles have solid stems.

## History
New York's ranking form for this group of honeysuckles lists their native range as Asia.

## How to handle it
Because birds spread honeysuckle seeds, control should start in late summer or early fall, before the berries are ready. Seedlings and young plants can be removed by hand when there are only a few, repeated every year. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
