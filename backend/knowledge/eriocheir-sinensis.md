## Summary
The Chinese mitten crab is a light-brown to olive-green crab with a shell up to 4 inches wide. Its claws are equal in size, with white tips and furry "mittens" of hair; a crab without hairy claws is probably not a mitten crab. It lives in both fresh and salt water and could have negative effects on native ecosystems. Small ones can be confused with the native Harris mud crab.

## History
The Chinese mitten crab is native to East Asia. The first one on the U.S. East Coast was found in Chesapeake Bay in 2006, and the first in New York was found in the Hudson River near the Tappan Zee Bridge in 2007. It has since been seen far up the Hudson and in Long Island Sound, and its importation and interstate transport are banned under the federal Lacey Act.

## Seasonal differences
Young crabs spend two to five years in freshwater rivers and streams, sometimes more than 50 miles inland. Adults then migrate downstream to salty estuaries to mate and spawn.

## How to handle it
Any sightings should be reported to iMapInvasives. NYIS says crabs that are collected should be destroyed and not returned live to the water. In New York City parks like Kissena, park rules forbid chasing, harassing, harming or removing any animal, nest or eggs.
