## Summary
Chinese lespedeza is a bushy plant 3 to 5½ feet tall with silvery-green, three-part leaves and stiff, sharp bristles on its older stems. It is the biggest threat to open areas like meadows, prairies, open woods, wetland edges and fields, where it crowds out native plants, forms stands of a single plant, and builds up a large supply of seeds in the soil. Its high tannin content makes it unappealing to livestock and most native wildlife.

## History
Chinese lespedeza is native to Asia and Australia. Federal and state agencies brought it to the U.S. in the late 1800s for bank stabilization, erosion control, soil improvement, mine reclamation and animal feed, and it has been escaping from those plantings for many years.

## Seasonal differences
Small creamy-white to pale-yellow flowers with purple centers bloom in summer, and fruits form in the fall. Animals such as bobwhite quail eat the seeds and spread them.

## How to handle it
Do not plant Chinese lespedeza. Hand-pulling is impractical because of its extensive root system; mowing plants while they are flowering, as low as possible, for two or three years in a row may help control it.
