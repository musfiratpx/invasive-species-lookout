## Summary
Japanese hop is a climbing or trailing vine with rough, mostly five-lobed leaves and downward-pointing prickles on its stems. A single vine can grow up to 35 feet in one season, letting it cover large areas and crowd out native plants. It likes sun, moisture and rich bare soil, and is most common along stream banks and floodplains, though it also grows on roadsides, old fields and forest edges.

## History
Japanese hop is native to eastern Asia. It was brought to the United States in the late 1800s as an Asian tonic and an ornamental vine, and it is still sold for those uses today. It is most common in the northeastern United States and eastern Canada.

## Seasonal differences
Japanese hop is an annual vine, growing fresh each year. Its small green flowers grow where the leaves meet the stem, with male and female flowers on separate plants: the female flowers hang in cone-shaped clusters. The small seeds are spread by wind and water.

## How to handle it
Do not buy, plant or transplant Japanese hop. The stems have prickles, so vines should only be hand-pulled while wearing gloves, long sleeves, long pants and shoes to avoid injury. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
