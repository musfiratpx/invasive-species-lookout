## Summary
The oriental weatherfish, also called the pond loach or dojo loach, is a small fish that burrows into soft bottoms and can survive low-oxygen water by gulping air. Its name comes from the way it becomes more active when air pressure changes, so it has long been called a sign of coming storms. It may reduce the aquatic insects that native fish eat, and may compete with the native eastern mudminnow for food.

## History
The oriental weatherfish is native to eastern Asia, from Siberia to northern Vietnam, including Japan. In the United States, early populations came from fish that escaped from a goldfish farm in California and an aquarium supply company in Michigan, and other introductions were presumably released aquarium fish. It is established in several states, including New York.

## How to handle it
Never release aquarium or pond fish into lakes, ponds or streams. NYS DEC suggests donating unwanted fish to a school, nature center, aquarium or zoo, returning them to the store, trading them with another hobbyist, or asking a veterinarian or pet store about humane options.
