## Summary
Morrow's honeysuckle is a shrub, usually 6 to 15 feet tall, with small, very fragrant tube-shaped flowers. It forms very dense thickets that crowd out and suppress native plants, and invasive honeysuckle berries are less fatty and nutritious than native ones, which can harm migrating birds that eat lots of them. Invasive honeysuckles have hollow stems, while native honeysuckles have solid stems.

## History
Morrow's honeysuckle is native to Japan. It was brought to Massachusetts in the 1860s and later released as an ornamental. It escaped cultivation and is easily spread by birds.

## Seasonal differences
It flowers from May to June with small, very fragrant tube-shaped flowers, creamy white to pink. Its quarter-inch red berries appear from mid-summer through early fall.

## How to handle it
Because birds spread the seeds, control should start in late summer or early fall, before the berries are ready. Seedlings and young plants can be removed by hand when there are only a few, repeated every year.
