## Summary
Narrowleaf bittercress is a small plant up to two feet tall in the mustard family, with finely divided leaves. It makes lots of seeds and can fertilize itself, so a single plant can quickly become a smothering colony that pushes out native plants, possibly including early spring wildflowers. It grows in rich, moist soil along ditches, streams and floodplains, and in the dappled shade of disturbed woods.

## History
Narrowleaf bittercress is native to Eurasia. In the United States it was first recorded in New Hampshire in 1916, and it now grows in at least 18 states.

## Seasonal differences
Its tiny white four-petaled flowers bloom from April to June, and seeds form from early May through early July. It finishes its life cycle and dies back shortly after midsummer.

## How to handle it
Hand-pulling or digging young plants works if it is done before the seeds form.
