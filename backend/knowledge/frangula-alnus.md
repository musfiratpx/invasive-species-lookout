## Summary
Alder buckthorn, also called glossy buckthorn, is a shrub that forms dense thickets whose shade wipes out native tree seedlings, saplings and wildflowers, and it can change which trees make up the forest canopy. In New York City it forms infestations of several acres, mostly under black locust trees, and it has been described as one of the most damaging non-native plants in Pelham Bay Park in the Bronx. Birds and mice eat its fruit, and the fruit can also float and be carried by water.

## History
Alder buckthorn is native to Eurasia. It was grown as an ornamental plant, though demand for it today is near zero. It is now found in all northeastern states and provinces except Delaware and Virginia.

## How to handle it
It resprouts vigorously when cut back, and burning usually just leads to more resprouting. Its seeds can stay alive in the soil for at least three years. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
