## Summary
Black locust is a tree that can form dense stands with heavy shade and little growing underneath. It is a nitrogen-fixing tree: it adds nitrogen to the soil, which can help other non-native plants invade, and studies found the soil stays changed for many years after the trees are removed. It can create a new tree layer in grasslands, fields and hedgerows, and spreads by sending up shoots from its roots as well as by seed.

## History
Black locust is native to the United States, in the central and southern Appalachians and the Ozarks, but it is not native to New York and has spread to all eastern states and Canadian provinces. It was widely planted in the past for many purposes, and New York State's own tree nursery still sells about 10,000 trees a year, mostly for reclaiming gravel pits.

## How to handle it
Black locust's bark, seeds and leaves can be fatal to livestock if eaten. It is hard to remove: taking out large trees takes major effort, and trees treated with herbicide can keep resprouting for several years.
