## Summary
Amur cork tree is a tree up to 40 feet tall with corky, grey, furrowed bark and bright yellow inner bark. Its leaves have 5 to 11 leaflets that smell of citrus or disinfectant when crushed. Its shade can keep native trees, shrubs and wildflowers from growing, it releases chemicals into the soil that harm native plants and soil microorganisms, and its fruit is less nutritious for birds than native fruit. It tolerates sun or deep shade, drought, flooding and pollution, so it does well in cities.

## History
Amur cork tree is native to East Asia. It was brought to the United States in the mid-1800s as a landscape tree. Female trees produce thousands of seeds, which birds spread.

## Seasonal differences
Male and female flowers grow on separate trees. Female trees bloom with clusters of small green flowers in late spring, which become drooping clusters of small, round, black fruit in mid-fall. The fruit stays on the trees through the winter.

## How to handle it
Managers remove female trees first to cut seed production. Seedlings and saplings can be hand-pulled; trees that are cut down will resprout unless the stump is treated, so control takes several years. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
