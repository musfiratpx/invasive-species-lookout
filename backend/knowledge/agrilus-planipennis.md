## Summary
The emerald ash borer is a small wood-boring beetle that feeds on, and eventually kills, all native ash trees. Once a tree is infested, most ash trees die within two to four years. Signs of infestation include a pale "blonding" of the trunk, dying branches in the crown, and D-shaped exit holes in the bark; where bark splits or falls off, S-shaped tunnels may show underneath. Adults have metallic-green wing covers and coppery red or purple bellies.

## History
The emerald ash borer comes from Asia. Its first North American populations were confirmed in 2002 in southeast Michigan and Windsor, Ontario, and it was likely brought there in the mid-1990s in ash wood used for shipping pallets and packing material. It was first discovered in New York State in 2009.

## Seasonal differences
Adult beetles begin coming out of trees in late May and can be seen through early September, and they are most common in June and July.

## How to handle it
New York limits moving firewood of any kind of tree to within 50 miles of where it came from, to slow the spread of pests like this one. An infested tree on your property does not have to be taken down unless it could be a hazard by falling.
