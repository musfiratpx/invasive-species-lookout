## Summary
The Chinese mystery snail is a large freshwater snail with a round shell of 6 to 7 whorls, olive green to brown or reddish brown in adults and lighter in young snails. It lives in slow or still fresh water with soft, muddy or silty bottoms. It is on New York's list of prohibited invasive species. It carries several parasites, including some that can infect people, and can pass one parasite on to native freshwater mussels. In experiments it reduced the number of a native snail, and in high numbers it changes how another native snail feeds. It can reach such high numbers that mass die-offs leave piles of shells on lake beaches.

## History
The Chinese mystery snail is native to Southeast Asia, Japan and eastern Russia. It was sold in a Chinese food market in San Francisco in the late 1800s, was collected in Boston as early as 1914, and was probably released from an aquarium into the Niagara River between 1931 and 1942.

## Seasonal differences
Females give birth to live, crawling young from about June through October in shallow water, then move to deeper water for the winter.

## Look-alikes
The Japanese mystery snail, also on New York's prohibited list, looks the same: a 2019 study found the two are genetically different but have no shell features that tell them apart, and both live in some New York lakes.
