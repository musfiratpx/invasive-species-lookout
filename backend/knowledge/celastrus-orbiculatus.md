## Summary
Oriental bittersweet is a woody vine that can climb up to 60 feet. It badly damages native plants by wrapping tightly around them (girdling), and its extra weight can break branches. It grows best in sun but can survive deep shade, and is found in disturbed woods, fields and roadsides. It can be confused with the native American bittersweet.

## History
Oriental bittersweet is native to eastern Asia. It was brought to North America as an ornamental plant in the mid-1800s.

## Seasonal differences
Flowers bloom where the leaves meet the stem in May and early June. In fall the vine produces bright reddish-orange fruit. Its rounded to oval leaves alternate along the stem.

## How to handle it
Individual vines can be pulled up by the roots and removed by hand, or cut and the stumps treated with herbicide. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
