## Summary
Japanese angelica tree is a fast-growing shrub or tree that can reach almost 40 feet, growing up to two feet in a season. It has very large, fern-like leaves and stems covered in sharp spines. It forms dense stands whose shade crowds out native shrubs and wildflowers, and one plant can quickly spread into a large colony. It grows in disturbed woods and edges such as old fields, thickets and hedgerows.

## History
Japanese angelica tree is native to East Asia. It is a relatively recent invader in the Northeast, so little is yet known about how fast it reproduces.

## Seasonal differences
It blooms in July and August with huge, flat clusters of white flowers, sometimes two feet across, which is when it is easiest to spot. Small purple to black berries generally appear in September.

## How to handle it
Young plants can be pulled or dug up before they make seed; larger plants tend to resprout from roots left in the ground. Managed areas should be checked for several years for new seedlings.
