## Summary
Black swallow-wort, also called black dog-strangling vine, is a climbing vine that forms large patches that grow over and smother native plants. Deer avoid eating it, and its tangled patches are hard to walk through. Monarch butterflies sometimes lay eggs on it, but the caterpillars cannot survive, and it can crowd out the milkweed monarchs need. Its small, star-shaped flowers are deep purple-black.

## History
Black swallow-wort is native to the western Mediterranean region. It may first have been brought to North America as an ornamental plant.

## Seasonal differences
Shoots come up in spring, the plants flower in early summer, and seed pods 1.5 to 3 inches long form in late July and early August. The seeds are carried by the wind, and a square meter of swallow-wort can produce 1,000 to 2,000 seeds a year.

## How to handle it
Its deep, fibrous roots make it hard to remove; if digging it up, get as much of the root crown as possible. For small patches, plants and seed pods can be bagged before they release seed. Mowing won't get rid of it, and mowing after seeds form can spread them.
