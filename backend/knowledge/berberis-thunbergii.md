## Summary
Japanese barberry is a thorny shrub up to 8 feet tall that forms dense thickets on forest floors and also grows in open and disturbed areas. It survives drought, full sun and full shade. It has been linked to Lyme disease: areas with large barberry infestations can have up to 90% more Lyme-disease-carrying ticks than areas with native shrubs, likely because it shelters the mice that young ticks feed on and keeps the air humid. It has also been linked to more invasive earthworms and more soil erosion.

## History
Japanese barberry is native to Asia. It was brought to the United States as an ornamental plant, and spreads both by seed and by growing new shoots.

## Seasonal differences
In April and May it has umbrella-shaped clusters of white flowers on the undersides of its branches. Its oblong red berries appear in September.

## How to handle it
The stems have thorns, single or in pairs. Smaller shrubs can be pulled up; larger shrubs and infestations are treated with herbicides, which are applied in early spring and late fall when native plants are dormant. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
