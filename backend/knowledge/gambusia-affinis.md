## Summary
The western mosquitofish is a small fish. Mosquitofish have had the biggest ecological impact of any of the small live-bearing fish people have introduced around the world. They were stocked widely to control mosquitoes, but scientific reviews have not found them especially effective at it. Because they are aggressive predators, they can harm small native fish through predation and competition, and in some places they have pushed out native fish.

## History
The western mosquitofish is native to the Mississippi River basin and Gulf Coast drainages, from central Indiana and Illinois south to the Gulf of Mexico. Because of their reputation for eating mosquito larvae, they were stocked widely around the world starting in the early 1900s; in 1905, mosquitofish reportedly from North Carolina were released into New Jersey waters to control mosquitoes. They generally don't tolerate cold well, so spreading in northern states is limited.

## How to handle it
Never release aquarium or pond fish into lakes, ponds or streams. NYS DEC suggests donating unwanted fish to a school, nature center, aquarium or zoo, returning them to the store, trading them with another hobbyist, or asking a veterinarian or pet store about humane options.
