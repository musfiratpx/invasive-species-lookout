## Summary
Broad-leaved pepperweed, also called tall pepperweed or perennial peppergrass, is a plant up to 5 feet tall with many stiff stems, waxy green leaves and dense clusters of tiny white flowers; it smells like horseradish. It forms large stands that crowd out native plants and reduce habitat for native birds, insects and other animals. In the Northeast it mainly invades salt marshes, competing with native marsh grasses, and sometimes grows along salted roads.

## History
Broad-leaved pepperweed is native to southern Europe and western Asia, and may have been brought to the United States by accident in shipments of sugar beet seed. In the East it is found mainly along the coasts of New York and New England.

## Seasonal differences
A rosette of leaves grows from late fall to early spring, and the plant flowers from late spring through summer and into fall. Old stems stay standing through the winter.

## How to handle it
Pulling or digging it out is not recommended because of its extensive roots, but mowing repeatedly before it sets seed may help hold it back. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
