## Summary
Jumping worms are earthworms, up to 8 inches long with a metallic brown color, that thrash wildly when picked up. Unlike most earthworms, the pale band around their body lies flat instead of being raised. They eat the organic matter in soil that feeds native plants, leaving soil grainy and damaging plant roots, and heavy infestations have been linked to declines in native plants, soil invertebrates, salamanders and birds. Amynthas species are prohibited in New York State.

## History
Jumping worms come from East Asia. They were brought to the southern United States in the late 1800s, likely on imported plants or horticultural materials.

## How to handle it
Prevention is the best tool. Don't buy or move jumping worms for composting, fishing or gardening, and avoid moving soil, mulch and plants from infested areas: their cocoons are smaller than a mustard seed and travel easily in soil and mulch. A single worm can start a new population, because they can reproduce without mating. In New York City parks like Kissena, park rules forbid chasing, harassing, harming or removing any animal, nest or eggs.
