## Summary
Canada thistle is a thistle 1.5 to 5 feet tall with small light-purple (sometimes white) flower heads and pale green leaves with many spines along their edges. Unlike other thistles, it spreads through an extensive sideways root system, forming dense patches of connected plants, with male and female flower heads on separate plants. It grows mainly in open, wet to moist places such as pastures, wetlands, floodplains, roadsides and ditches. It pushes out native plants, lowering plant and animal diversity, and it also reduces farm crop yields and pasture productivity.

## History
Despite its name, Canada thistle is native to Eurasia. It was brought to North America in shipments of agricultural seed, and it spreads by its roots and by people moving it around.

## How to handle it
Canada thistle spreads mostly through its roots, so mowing can hold it back but will not usually get rid of it. Infestations often need several control methods together and ongoing monitoring, and the whole patch needs treatment to stop it from resprouting.
