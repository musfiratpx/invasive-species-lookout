## Summary
Small carpetgrass is a low, sprawling annual grass that grows up to about a foot and a half tall. Its stems root where they touch the ground, and its short, broad, bright green leaves (up to about 3 inches long) wrap around the stem. It grows fast and competes very well in moist soil, where it can form dense stands that crowd out native plants and may change which plants grow there. It does best in moist, sunny places such as stream banks, the edges of ponds and lakes, wet meadows and other open, damp, disturbed ground.

## History
Small carpetgrass is native to Asia (NYIS lists eastern Asia including India, New Guinea and Japan, plus Australia). No one knows exactly when or how it reached North America, but its patchy spread suggests it arrived by accident several times, perhaps as early as the late 1800s. It now grows in at least 25 states.

## Seasonal differences
It is an annual, so each plant lives for one year and makes a lot of seed. Its flowers appear toward the end of summer, in a cluster of up to 10 finger-like spikes that can be greenish to purple.

## How to handle it
Hand pulling works if it is done before the plant makes seed, and mowing or cutting works if it is started when the plant begins to flower, not earlier. Larger patches may also need herbicide. Because new seedlings can come up, treated spots should be checked again for at least a year. Seeds spread by wind, water and people, including on clothing and in moved soil.
