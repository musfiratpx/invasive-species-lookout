## Summary
Eurasian watermilfoil is an underwater plant rooted in lake bottoms that grows quickly into dense beds and surface mats, usually in water 3 to 13 feet deep. The mats shade out native water plants early in the season, leading to fewer invertebrates and native fish, smaller fish spawning areas and slower fish growth, and native waterfowl avoid feeding in it. It also makes boating and swimming difficult, and control costs New York more than half a million dollars a year.

## History
Eurasian watermilfoil is native to Eurasia. It is now a major water invader across much of North America; because it looks so much like native watermilfoils, reports from before 1940 may have been mistaken identifications.

## How to handle it
Cutting and harvesting only gives short-term relief and releases plant fragments that can spread it to new areas, and herbicides can harm other plants and fish. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
