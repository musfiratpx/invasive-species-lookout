## Summary
Cypress spurge is a low perennial, about a foot tall, with bright yellow-green leaves and flowers. Its stems and leaves ooze a milky white sap when damaged. It spreads quickly from an extensive, deeply buried root system and often grows in large clumps, and once it takes hold it is extremely difficult to control. It grows best on dry sites such as roadsides, old fields, pastures and woodland edges.

## History
Cypress spurge is native to Eurasia. No one knows exactly how it reached the United States, but it was likely brought as a garden plant in the mid-1800s. It now grows in at least 42 states.

## Seasonal differences
Its small, lime-green to yellow flower clusters turn orange-red to brown as they age. Each plant can produce up to 900 seeds, which may stay alive in the soil for up to 20 years.

## How to handle it
The plant's sap is toxic to cattle and horses. Pulling or digging only works if the whole deep root system comes out, and mowing usually makes it resprout unless it is done at least monthly.
