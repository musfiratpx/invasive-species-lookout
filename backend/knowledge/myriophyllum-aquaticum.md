## Summary
Parrot feather is a water plant named for its feather-like leaves, which grow in rings of 4 to 6 around the stem, both under the water and sticking up above it. It roots in the bottom of shallow ponds, slow streams and lake edges, and its stems can reach about five feet long. Dense mats can take over small ponds, slow the flow of water and make flooding worse, shade out native water plants that waterfowl eat, lower the oxygen in the water, which may harm fish, and give mosquitoes more places to breed.

## History
Parrot feather is native to the Amazon River basin and other parts of South America. It came to North America as an ornamental pond plant that escaped. Only female plants are found outside South America, so it is not known to make seeds here; it spreads when pieces break off and start new plants. Fragments can be carried by water and waterfowl, on boats, and through plant sales.

## Seasonal differences
New shoots grow each spring from underground stems once the water warms up. Flowers usually appear in spring or fall, and the plant usually dies back to its underground stems in the autumn.

## How to handle it
Any piece of the plant can grow into a new one, and removing it with chemicals or by hand or machine has worked only partly. Never dump aquarium or water garden plants into streams, ponds or lakes, and don't compost them. NYS DEC suggests donating unwanted plants to a school, nature center, aquarium or zoo, returning them to the store, or swapping them with another hobbyist.
