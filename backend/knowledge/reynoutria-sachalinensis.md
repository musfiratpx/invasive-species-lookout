## Summary
Giant knotweed is assessed together with Japanese knotweed on New York's official ranking form, which ranks them Very High. It comes up early and grows fast, up to about 3 inches a day, forming stands that smother everything beneath them. Its dead stems last through the winter, smothering plants below and creating a fire and flood hazard.

## History
Giant knotweed is native to Asia (China, Japan and Korea). It has been planted occasionally for erosion control and sold as a garden plant, and it is now found throughout New York State.

## Seasonal differences
It comes up early in the season and grows quickly; last year's dead stems stay standing through the winter.

## How to handle it
Pieces of its stems and roots can grow into new plants, and it is extremely difficult to get rid of once established. Seeds and plant fragments can be carried in soil and compost. In New York City parks like Kissena, cutting, killing or removing plants without permission from NYC Parks is against park rules, so report it instead.
